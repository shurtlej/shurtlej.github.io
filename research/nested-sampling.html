Nested Sampling

Jakob Shurtleff · Dr. Lance Nelson

Project page in progress. Results and figures will be added as simulations finish.

Overview

Investigating the material properties of silicon at high temperatures and pressures is time-consuming, leaving limited experimental data on its phase and crystal structure beyond certain pressures. Nested sampling is a computational method for approximating the partition (Z) function of silicon, which can then be used to analyze its properties across temperatures. However, nested sampling relies on millions of energy evaluations: accurate calculations are computationally expensive, while fast approximations can lose accuracy. The core challenge is optimizing the trade-off between computational cost and accuracy. Because we suspect the Stillinger-Weber potential will not accurately model silicon at high pressures, we are testing whether the Tersoff potential can replicate known experimental results below 10 GPa before using it to predict the phase diagram at higher pressures.

Methods

We run simulations of silicon using nested sampling with 256 atoms initially distributed across a large diffuse volume. The simulation initializes 50 random walkers and culls the highest-energy walker at each iteration while cloning and moving a lower-energy walker. This iteratively steps down the potential energy surface to approximate the accessible volume beneath each energy contour in configuration space. We target convergence around 300 K, since lower-energy volume contributions below that point add little to the 1000 K to 1500 K range where we analyze specific heat.
